This project looks at the gut bacteria of 446 adults from Colombian cities and asks two simple questions: do people fall into separate groups, or is it one smooth spectrum? And what changes most along that spectrum, their metabolic health or their diet? We used a math method that studies the “shape” of data to map how similar people’s gut bacteria are to each other.

We found one smooth spectrum with no clear groups, and metabolic health lined up with it much more strongly than diet did. At one end, people with better metabolic markers had more Akkermansia bacteria. At the other end, there was more Prevotella and Escherichia. This shows a link, not cause and effect. The full report and slides (in Spanish) are in the repo. 

Made as a final project for a *geometry and topology for data science* course.
